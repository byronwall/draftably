Sequence diagrams
Sequence diagrams

layout root sequence draws an exchange between participants. Every node is a participant, drawn in a row across the top in file order (or the order an order root line gives), with a dashed lifeline below it. Every edge is a message, drawn on its own row in the order the edge lines appear, as a straight arrow from the sender's lifeline to the receiver's, so the file reads top to bottom as the exchange does. A message's label sits just above its arrow, a step larger than other labels, and wraps to about 170px; a self-message's label sits beside its loop. Participants are spaced for the wrapped labels, and a short participant title gets a box only as wide as it needs.

kind=async draws a dashed message, the usual mark of a reply. A message from a participant to itself draws a short loop. Participants are spaced so each message's label fits between them. Position refinements do not apply.

The diagram is drawn on a framed panel, and each participant is outlined in its color with a thin stripe of it along the top, so the row of headers stays quiet. A top-level group gathers participants (in=group) into a lightly tinted column band behind their lifelines, with its title above them; its participants stay together where its first one falls in the order. A phase lists messages that belong together and draws them as a row band across the panel, its label in a tab at the left; alternate phases are tinted, so neighbours read apart:
