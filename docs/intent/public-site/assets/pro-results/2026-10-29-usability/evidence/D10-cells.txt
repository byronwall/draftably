Cells

layout group cells draws a group as one object made of parts, like the parts of a request in order. The group's nodes are its cells: they touch and share borders, in declaration order (or the group's order line), under a band that holds the group's title. There is no gap and no inset.

Cells form one row by default; direction=down makes one column. Every cell in a row has the row's height; every cell in a column has the column's width.
columns=N makes a grid, such as a table: cells fill each row of N columns in order. Each column is as wide as its widest cell. span=N on a cell covers N columns; a cell that does not fit the rest of its row starts the next row. A spanning cell wider than its columns widens the last of them.
A cell is as wide as its title on one line, from 64 to 240 pixels, and wraps past that. A size line sets it exactly. A title band wider than the cells stretches the last cell.
A cell has no color of its own unless it names one, so the band carries the group's color.
shape=marker marks a seam, such as a cache point. It takes no room: a bar in the theme's text color cuts the object, title band included, before the next cell, and its label sits above. Lines reach a marker from below.
Lines can start or end at any cell. A line meets a cell on a side that lies on the group's outline, facing the line's other end; a cell's top faces the title band, so it is never used. A cell with no such side, inside a grid, is reached across the cells in the way: the line enters the outline facing its other end, is drawn over the cells, and ends with a dot at the cell. from= and to= still choose a side.
A marker needs one row or one column: it cannot cut a grid.
A cell stays in its place. near, nudge, stay and pin on a cell are reported and ignored; move the group instead, by its title band. An engine on the root moves the group as one block.
In the viewer, dragging a cell and dropping it among the others reorders the group: it writes the group's order line. A marker keeps its place before the cell it preceded. The inspector's Span field writes span=.
Cells are nodes, so colors, links, count= and change marks work on them.

Byte and bit layouts

unit=byte or unit=bit on a cells layout draws an on-disk or on-the-wire structure, like the figures in the PNG spec or an RFC packet header. Every column is one unit of the same width, so a field's width is its size.

columns= sets the units in each row: 16 by default for bytes, 32 for bits. span=N is the field's size in units; a field without one is one unit.
A field longer than the rest of its row runs on into the next rows. Its title sits in its longest piece; the other pieces show it after an ellipsis.
span=var is a field of variable length. It fills the rest of its row, or a whole row, and its sides are drawn broken.
A ruler numbers the units between the title band and the first row. ruler=off hides it.
unit-width= sets the width of one unit in pixels: 48 for bytes and 24 for bits by default. Titles wrap to fit their field, so give one-bit flags short titles.
Markers do not apply to byte or bit layouts.
