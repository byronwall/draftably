Themes and colors

The file owns the diagram's appearance. A theme line picks a preset and may override its diagram-wide values; theme-color lines change or add colors. Every value is optional: anything not written comes from the preset, and a file with no theme line uses Drafting.

theme attributes: padding-x and padding-y (4–48, the space between a node's edge and its title), radius (0–24, rectangle corners), title-font and annotation-font (sans, serif or mono), and canvas, text and connection colors. Colors are hex values in double quotes: # outside quotes starts a comment.

Switching themes keeps the layout: nodes are placed on slots sized in Drafting (with the file's own overrides) and drawn at the theme's size in their slot, centred along the reading direction with each row keeping its top. A slot grows only where the theme's node is larger than the gap around it can absorb, so rows and order always hold.

Families and shades. Built-in families: primary and secondary (the theme's own accents), slate, stone, blue, sky, teal, mint, green, amber, orange, red, rose and violet. Each has shades 50 100 200 300 400 500 600 700 800 900 950; lower numbers are lighter and higher numbers darker in every theme, dark ones included. Shades 50–300 are pale fills with dark text and 400–950 are fills with white text, each held to WCAG contrast targets (7:1 and 4.5:1). Warm families (amber, orange, and a theme accent of those hues) keep 400 and 500 bright with dark text, since a warm fill dark enough for white text turns brown; their 600 and darker carry white text. A theme may tune a family's hue: Signal draws green, orange, red and rose as Okabe–Ito's bluish green, orange, vermilion and reddish purple. A node that names a color without a shade uses 500; a group uses a quiet surface shade (100, or 900 in Blueprint). Shade is chosen per object: color=auto inherits a group's family, never its shade. A node with neither color nor shade uses the theme's neutral surface.

Legend. legend family "meaning" adds a row to the diagram's color key, in file order: a swatch of that family (shade= picks one; 500 by default) and its meaning. The viewer shows the key in the canvas corner, in the diagram's colors; inspect --svg and exports draw it under the drawing. Each family and shade may appear once. A legend never moves anything. check warns when a swatch's shade matches none of its family's nodes (legend-shade), such as the default 500 for nodes drawn at shade=200.

Drawing keeps an object apart from a container of its own family. A group inside a same-family group steps one shade toward emphasis per level (100, 200, 300 in light themes; 900, 800, 700 in Blueprint), so nested frames read as layers rather than one fill. A node whose shade is within two steps of its container's moves three steps past it. check notes a file with more than four accent families (many-colors; slate and stone don't count) and a member that names its container's family (color-matches-container).

theme-color family shade=N fill= stroke= ink= sets one shade (shade defaults to 500). For a built-in family any of the three may be given and the rest come from the theme. A new family, such as brand, defines each shade it uses with all three. Referencing a family or shade that is not defined is an error on the referencing line. Each family and shade may be declared once.

Padding and title type change node sizes and so the layout; colors and shades never move anything. Choosing a theme in the viewer's Appearance menu rewrites the theme line, clears the old preset's overrides and keeps your own theme-color families.

Titles wrap using width tables for the bundled fonts (Inter, Source Serif 4 and JetBrains Mono, under the SIL Open Font License), so the compiler, the viewer and inspect --svg break every title at the same place. A word too long for a line breaks where an identifier's parts meet (annotation / Commands, src/ / viz/), and a node without a size grows wider, up to twice its default width, rather than break a word in the middle. Exported SVG files embed the fonts they use.
